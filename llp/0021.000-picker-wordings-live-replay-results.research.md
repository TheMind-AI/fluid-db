## Summary

Round 14, on LLP 0018.000's benchmark (18 people's real conversations, OpenAI only, aggregates only), with real Jev
on the maintainer's own data.
- **The picker needed to see the fact.** Asking the same closed question on whole windows instead of their first
  800 characters: +4.8 points of hit@5 (71.9%).
  - An LLM ranking the 20 candidates as a list: 74.0%.
  - Real Jev picks as well as the OpenAI model, in 0.33 s against 2.8 s.
- **Keeping every wording fixed the merge.** Linked statements (each with its repeat count) score as well as raw
  statements (66.3%) and +3.4 over one merged wording.
- **A new best memory:** the kept-current dossier, linked statements and windows, both ranked by the LLM
  (`conv_best2`).
  - **73.0%, against 70.0% for reading the whole history (+2.9, CI 0.9 to 5.0):** the first significant win over
    it, at a quarter of the tokens.
- **But in a live conversation, the memory mostly goes unused.**
  - The assistant wrote Mind's next message at 590 real moments.
  - Even with the best memory, it showed it already knew what the person was about to re-tell in 5.1% of
    messages, and referred back after the re-telling in 9.5%. Without memory: 3.1% and 3.1%.
- **Telling the assistant to use its memory multiplies that by five:** 17.3% before and 45.8% after, with invented
  memories still at 1 in 98.
  - Most of it is related earlier detail.
  - Strictly the same fact, acknowledged as told before: 10.2%.
  - The bottleneck has moved from recall to use.
- **The round cost $11.08.**

## Part A: a better picker

`deprecated/python/lab/bench/replay.py pick`. hit@5 over windows, from meaning search's top 20. The top 20 hold the earlier mention
88.8% of the time.

**18 accounts, 1,176 moments (the OpenAI model in Jev's place):**

| picker | cue | reactive | proactive | all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no picker: meaning's top 5 | 63.8 | 65.3 | 51.8 | 60.3 |
| P0: closed question, first 800 characters | 73.5 | 72.2 | 55.6 | 67.1 |
| **P1: closed question, whole window** | 78.8 | 78.1 | 58.7 | **71.9** |
| P2: closed question, the window's statements (a card) | 75.8 | 73.7 | 59.4 | 69.6 |
| P3: the LLM ranks the 20 as a list | 84.2 | 76.3 | 61.5 | **74.0** |
| ceiling: in the top 20 | 92.1 | 92.6 | 81.6 | 88.8 |

- **The contrasts:**
  - P1 - P0: +4.8 (2.4 to 7.1)
  - P2 - P0: +2.6 (0.2 to 4.9)
  - P1 - P3: -2.1 (-4.3 to -0.1)
  - P0 - no picker: +6.8
- **The maintainer's data, 207 moments:**
  - P0 62.3, P1 63.8, P2 63.3, P3 66.2, ceiling 73.4.
  - Jev and the OpenAI model score the same on every picker (+0.0, +0.0, +1.0).
  - Jev takes 0.33-0.36 s per decision against 2.8-3.1 s, even on whole windows.
  - Whole windows gain little here (+1.4). His windows average 820 characters, so the 800-character cut rarely hid
    anything. The 18 accounts' windows average about 1,200, and 2,200 in the account with the most questions.

## Part C: the live replay

`deprecated/python/lab/bench/replay.py live`, `live_active`, `strict`.
- 295 re-told statements (at most 30 per account), 2 moments each.
- 4 memories with the prompt that says to use memory when it helps; 2 with a directive to check the memory and say
  so explicitly.

| memory, prompt | knows before (lenient) | refers back after (lenient) | the same fact, after (strict) | invents | tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 3.1% | 3.1% | 2.4% | 0 of 98 | 876 |
| the product's injected memory | 4.7% | 5.1% | 3.4% | 0 of 98 | 2,398 |
| `conv_best2` | 5.1% | 9.5% | 4.7% | 1 of 98 | 9,567 |
| the whole history | 4.7% | 8.5% | 2.0% | 0 of 98 | 27,561 |
| the product's memory, **directive** | 12.2% | 42.4% | 7.5% | 1 of 98 | 2,431 |
| `conv_best2`, **directive** | **17.3%** | **45.8%** | **10.2%** | 1 of 98 | 9,598 |

- **The contrasts:**
  - after, `conv_best2` - none: +6.4 (3.1 to 9.8)
  - after, `conv_best2` - the product's memory: +4.4 (1.0 to 7.8)
  - after, the directive's gain for `conv_best2`: +36.3 (30.2 to 42.4)
  - before: +12.2 (7.8 to 16.6)
  - strictly, the directive's gain: +5.4 (2.0 to 9.2)
  - strictly, the whole history - `conv_best2`: -2.7 (-5.1 to -0.7)
- **The lenient judges count related earlier detail.** Asked strictly whether the reply acknowledges the same fact
  as told before, only a tenth of the replies do, even with the best memory and the directive.
- **Reading everything is the worst at using it.** With the whole history (27k tokens), the assistant referred back
  to the same fact in 2.0% of replies, no more than with no memory. A picked memory puts the relevant item in front
  of it.
- **The judge checks**, on the maintainer's cut (own2409), by hand:
  - The first judges counted echoes of the current conversation as memory. They were rewritten to see the
    conversation.
  - After the rewrite, 26 of 29 verdicts agreed with my labels (1 uncertain). All 8 positives for `conv_best2` were
    right.
  - The first 16 positives under the directive were genuine references to earlier conversations, often to a
    related fact rather than the same one. That is what the strict re-judge separates.

## What this changes (LLP 0015)

- **Recall is no longer the bottleneck; use is.**
  - The memory holds 73% of what people re-tell.
  - The assistant, as prompted here, acknowledges the same fact in at most a tenth of its replies.
  - Two changes help: a directive prompt (+36 points of referring back, related or not), and memory picked for the
    turn rather than the whole history.
- **The next part is a re-telling detector,** a closed decision made for Jev: at each turn, is the person telling
  me something already in my memory, and which item?
  - On yes, the assistant gets that item and an instruction to acknowledge it.
  - This is the step between "the memory has it" and "the conversation changes".
- **For the picker:** whole windows, not cuts. On a product's every turn, Jev's closed questions on whole windows
  (71.8% end to end, 0.33 s per decision) are the affordable choice. An LLM ranking the list is 1-2 points better,
  and slower.
- **Keep every wording; count the repeats.**

## Deviations

- **The live judges were rewritten once,** after the smoke test and the first check on the maintainer's cut showed
  they counted echoes of the current conversation. All reported numbers use the rewritten judges.
- **The directive-prompt arms and the strict re-judge are exploratory,** added after Part C's first results.
- **`conv_best2` uses P3 by the rule fixed in Part B** (the highest pooled hit@5). The Jev-picked variant is
  exploratory.
- **The live conversation is reconstructed:** the person's messages and the last question of each of Mind's turns.
- **The stand-in assistant's prompt is not Mind's.**

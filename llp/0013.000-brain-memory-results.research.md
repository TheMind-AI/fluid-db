**Type:** Research
**Status:** Draft
**Systems:** Core, Reads, Writes, Jev, Research
**Author:** Adam Zvada / Claude
**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Related:** LLP 0013, LLP 0002, LLP 0008, LLP 0011, LLP 0012.000

## Summary

Seven stores were built from the round-6 database (5,050 messages, 2.5 simulated years) as swappable parts, and 22
configurations were run on 94 questions in 12 memory types, plus 36 held-out questions written after the system was
frozen.
- **Several stores beat one, by about 9 points.**
  - Reading all seven stores beats the round-4 FluidDB reader (database plus log search) by +9.7 points on the held-out
    questions (95% CI +1.4 to +19.4) and by +9.0 on the first, untuned run (CI +2.1 to +16.5).
  - With Jev routing to about two stores per question, the gain is +4.2 held-out and +4.3 untuned, and neither is
    significant.
  - The development set, tuned on over two passes, shows +16.5 and +13.3: that is how much tuning adds.
- **The stores that matter compute something.**
  - Facts (SQL over the tables): removing them drops aggregates from 75% to 12.5%.
  - Routines (regularities computed by code): removing them drops routine questions from 81% to 44%; adding them
    alone to facts + log gives +9.6 overall.
  - Periods (where the user lived when, month summaries): adding them alone gives +6.9 overall.
  - Episodes, intentions and preferences mostly repeat what facts + log already hold. Removing one costs about 6
    points or less on its own questions; adding one alone gives -0.5 to +2.6.
- **Full context loses to the memory.** The whole log in the prompt (122k tokens per question) scores 78.6% on a
  35-question subset. The Jev-routed memory scores 91.4% on the same questions with 1.6k tokens, 76× fewer. Full
  context can't count (0% on aggregates), mixes up days, and reports finished reminders as open.
- **Jev routes as well as an LLM, 11× faster:** 0.28 s vs 3.1 s per question, with equal accuracy (91.0% vs 89.4%
  on the dev set, 86.1% vs 86.1% held-out). Lowering Jev's threshold from 0.5 to 0.3 adds 2-3 points for 45% more
  tokens.
- **As the decider for consolidation, Jev matches GPT-6 Luna on closed semantic questions** (memory aspects,
  answer verification) at 4-6× lower cost and 7-33× lower latency. It is worse calibrated on row faithfulness (AUROC
  0.68 vs 0.81) and slightly worse on routing (0.89 vs 0.92).
- **Forgetting was incomplete in the round-4 database.**
  - Both forgotten phone numbers were still in its log, and Marco's number was still a row.
  - Erasure now runs first in consolidation. An audit finds none of the forgotten strings in any of the seven stores.
- **Consolidation costs $0.13 once for 5,050 messages;** answering costs $0.0004-0.0009 per question. The round cost
  about $1.6.

## What was built

Everything is in `deprecated/python/lab/memory/` (LLP 0013#design). One append-only log feeds every store; the round-6 database
provides the facts.

| store | human memory | built by | size | build cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `log` | sensory buffer, verbatim trace | the messages; BM25 with light stemming | 5,050 messages | – |
| `facts` | semantic | FluidDB (rounds 4-6): the deterministic reader, plus matching rows and history | 4,685 readable lines | – |
| `episodes` | episodic | the log cut into days (before 4 am counts as the evening before), one gist per day by Luna | 911 days | $0.048 |
| `routines` | procedural, habits | code over the facts: days, hours, places, partners, typical amounts and durations, per residence period | 37 statements | $0 |
| `intentions` | prospective | Jev gate → Luna extraction → code tracks done, cancelled, moved, past | 31 from 174 candidates | $0.004 |
| `preferences` | evaluative | Jev gate → Luna extraction (holder, stance, kind, target) | 115 from 139 candidates | $0.003 |
| `periods` | autobiographical | residence periods from the user's row and its history; milestones (Jev); month summaries of the day gists | 2 periods, 22 milestones, 30 months | $0.011 |

- **Consolidation ("sleep"), in order:**
  1. Erase what the user asked to forget ([Forgetting](#forgetting)).
  2. One Jev pass asks five yes/no questions of every message: intention, done, cancel, preference, milestone.
     That is 25,250 answers in 316 requests, for $0.065.
  3. Each store takes what it needs, at its own threshold.
- **Build and rebuild.** The whole build cost $0.13 (`deprecated/python/lab/results/brain_audit.json`). Every model call is cached, so
  rebuilding from the cache takes 3.2 s.
- **Swappable parts:**
  - `Store`: `build`, `read → [Evidence]`
  - `Decider`: Jev or an LLM, same interface
  - `Router`: all, oracle, or a decider's yes/no per store, with a threshold
  - `Answerer`: evidence in a fixed store order under a 24k-character budget, one Luna call; or the round-4 hybrid
  - An experiment is a config: stores + router + answerer (+ lesion). `deprecated/python/lab/memory/configs.py` holds 22 of them, one
    line each.

## Results

Grading is by the Jev judge against gold (LLP 0009). "I don't know" is correct only for metamemory and forgotten
questions. Configs:
- **Baselines:** `hybrid_r4` is the round-4 reader: deterministic when trusted, else the Luna agent with SQL and log
  search. `facts_only`, `log_rag` and `facts_log` use the new answerer with one or two stores.
- **`brain_*`** use all seven stores, differing only in the router.

### Which stores matter: lesions and additions (H2)

- **Lesion:** `brain_all` with one store returning nothing (necessity).
- **Addition:** facts + log + one store (sufficiency).
- Both on the dev set.

| store | its question types | lesion: own types | lesion: other types (mean abs.) | addition: overall vs `facts_log` 79.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| facts | current, past, aggregate | -29.2 (aggregate 75 → 12.5) | 6.3 | – |
| log | source | 0 | 2.8 | – |
| episodes | episode, when | -6.3 | 0.6 | 82.4 (+2.6) |
| routines | routine | **-37.4** (81 → 44) | 3.8 | **89.4 (+9.6)**: routine 38 → 75, period 38 → 81 |
| intentions | prospective | -6.2 | 1.3 | 79.3 (-0.5) |
| preferences | preference | 0 | 2.1 | 81.9 (+2.1) |
| periods | period | 0 | 3.4 | **86.7 (+6.9)**: period 38 → 75 |

- **Only routines dissociate cleanly** (own ≥ 20 down, others < 5).
- **Facts is a hub.** Every type leans on it, so its lesion also moves other types by 6.3 points.
- **The rest are redundant.**
  - The log's documents are also in facts and in the episodes' messages.
  - Prospective questions are served by FluidDB's `tasks` and `events` tables.
  - Ratings are in `movies` and `books`.
  - Periods can be pieced together from the routines' "while living in …" statements and the history.
- **What cannot be pieced together from retrieved text is computation over many rows:**
  - totals and counts (facts)
  - weekday and hour distributions, typical values (routines)
  - what was going on in a stretch of time (periods, months)

### Full context (H4)

On a stratified subset (35 questions: positions 0, 3 and 6 of each type), the whole erased log (272k characters) goes
into the answer prompt.

|---|---|---|

- **Aggregates: 0 of 3.**
  - "How many times did I get coffee at Doubleshot?" 81, truth 93.
  - "Dinners with Eva in 2026": 10, truth 11.
  - Coffee spend in 2025: "I don't know".

### Routing (H3)

| router | dev | held-out | stores per question | median routing latency | AUROC vs the oracle's stores |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 94.1 | 91.7 | 7 | 0 | – |
| Jev, p ≥ 0.3 | 93.1 | 88.9 | 3.2 | 0.28 s | – |
| Jev, p ≥ 0.5 | 91.0 | 86.1 | 2.1 | 0.28 s | 0.887 |
| GPT-6 Luna | 89.4 | 86.1 | 2.8 | 3.14 s | 0.921 |
| oracle (by type) | 86.7 | 87.5 | 1.9 | 0 | – |

- **Jev asks one yes/no per store about the question and each store's description,** in one request. It never sees
  the question types.
- **Jev's probabilities make the threshold a recall knob.** At 0.3 it reads 3.2 stores, and closes half to two-thirds
  of the gap to reading everything, at 61% of the tokens.

### Jev as the decider (H5)

The same decisions, in the same batches, by Jev and by GPT-6 Luna (structured output with a stated probability).
`deprecated/python/lab/results/brain_deciders.json`.

| decision set | n | Jev AUROC | Luna AUROC | Jev / Luna cost | Jev / Luna median latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| memory aspects (5 per message, hand labels) | 312 messages | 0.997-1.000 | 0.990-1.000 | $0.004 / $0.024 | 0.37 s / 12.4 s |
| answer verification (the deterministic reader's answers, graded) | 69 | 0.952 | 0.932 | $0.0013 / $0.0052 | 0.27 s / 1.87 s |
| row faithfulness (round-6 truth, 163 wrong) | 500 rows | 0.676 | **0.813** | $0.0055 / $0.033 | 0.30 s / 7.47 s |
| routing (vs the oracle's stores) | 94 questions | 0.887 | **0.921** | – | 0.28 s / 3.14 s |

- **Equal on closed semantic judgments:** is this a reminder or a preference, does this result answer the question?
- **Worse where the judgment compares details:** does this row record the message's values? Luna finds 46 wrong rows
  in its top 50, Jev 42.
- **Jev's scale is conservative for some questions.** At p ≥ 0.5 it finds 69% of intentions (Luna 94%), though it
  ranks them almost perfectly. Thresholds must be chosen from data: 0.15 for the intention gate keeps 97% of them.
- **A parsing bug first made Jev look far better.**
  - Luna renamed the keys in 64 of 69 verifier answers ("q1"), and reported confidence in its own answer rather than
    P(yes): "no, 1.0".
  - The first parse read the first as 0 and the second as P(yes) = 1.0, giving the verifier AUROC 0.54.
  - Matching answers by position and making the probability agree with the stated yes/no fixed it
    (`deprecated/python/lab/memory/deciders.py:match`, `p_yes`).
  - The LLM router ran again with the fix: 18 questions changed stores, and accuracy stayed at 89.4%.

## Hypotheses

Written before the experiment (LLP 0013#hypotheses).
- **H1 (several stores beat one by ≥ 10): not supported for the Jev-routed memory.**
  - It holds on the tuned dev set (+13.3 over `hybrid_r4`, +36.7 over `log_rag`).
  - Held-out and untuned, the gain is +4.2 and +4.3, not significant.
  - Reading all stores gives +9.7 held-out (significant) and +9.0 untuned.
- **H2 (a double dissociation for ≥ 3 stores): refuted.**
  - Only routines dissociate.
  - The stores are redundant rather than modular ([lesions](#which-stores-matter-lesions-and-additions-h2)).
- **H3 (Jev within 5 points of the oracle and LLM routers at ≤ 1/5 of the latency): supported.**
  - Jev is at or above the LLM router on both sets, and within 1.4 points of the oracle, at 1/11 of the latency.
- **H4 (full context within 5 points or above, at ≥ 10× the tokens): refuted.** Full context is 12.8 points below
  the Jev-routed memory, at 76× the tokens.
- **H5 (Jev within 3 points of Luna, AUROC ≥ Luna's, ≥ 3× cheaper and faster): partly supported.**
  - It holds on aspects and verification.
  - Its AUROC is lower on row faithfulness and routing.
  - It is ≥ 4× cheaper and ≥ 7× faster everywhere.

## Development

Two passes on the dev set before freezing. First-pass numbers: `hybrid_r4` 76.6, `facts_log` 72.3, `brain_jev`
80.9, `brain_all` 85.6. Every fix is generic; none names a question.
- **Consolidation labels.**
  - One 6-way Jev choice per message labelled "climbing at Smíchoff with Tom tonight" as an intention (88
    "intentions", most of them climbs) and "nvm" as a cancel.
  - It also dropped a second aspect of a message: "She loves orchids", inside a birthday message.
  - Five independent yes/no questions per message fixed both: orchids p = 0.98.
  - At 0.5, though, they missed a quarter of calendar invites, whence the 0.15 gate.
- **Status tracking.**
  - A bare "nvm" closed a dentist appointment through a title the extractor had invented. Matching now uses the
    closing message's own words.
  - A possessive "'s" linked "forget Marco's number" to "get SF driver's license". Only words of ≥ 3 letters count
    now.
  - "done: cancel Czech phone plan" was extracted as a cancel. Jev's done-vs-cancel probabilities now decide.
  - Later appointments move earlier ones ("Lucas moved our call"), and follow up earlier plans.
  - All 31 statuses were checked by hand against the storyline.
- **Retrieval.**
  - Light stemming: "groceries/grocery", "meetings/meeting".
  - A table's single-valued categories name its routines ("workouts (run, km)").
  - Durations read as m:ss.
  - Preferences say what kind of thing they judge.
  - The preference extractor was told that the holder can be someone else: one re-extraction had lost "Mom loves
    orchids".
- **Answerer.**
  - The evidence goes in a fixed store order: the routed order had flipped answers.
  - Four general rules were added to the prompt:
    - a report dates its event
    - "open, not done yet" means not done
    - list a whole day
    - a similar name is someone else (it had answered Dad's birthday with David's)
- **Benchmark.** One prospective question, "Did I renew my Czech ID card?", presumed a "no" the memory cannot know. It
  was reworded to ask what happened to the reminder, for all configs, before the second pass.

## Forgetting

- **What was left behind.** The round-4 database, used by rounds 5 and 6, still held:
  - "+420 602 555 777" in the message about Jan Dvořák (only his name had been redacted)
  - Marco's message with his number
  - Marco's number as a `contact_methods` row: the planner had deleted his "barber" relationship instead
- **The ledger's "forgetting can be made complete" (round 2) was not applied at scale.**
- **Erasure is now the first consolidation step** (`deprecated/python/lab/memory/stores.py:erase_forgotten`):
  1. For each "forget …" message, Luna reads the earlier messages that may be about it. It names either whole
     messages to erase ("forget everything about X") or exact strings.
  2. Code erases them from the log, the history and every row. Rows made only from an erased message are deleted:
     Rohlik (from Jan's message), Marco's number and Marco.
  3. Strings found in more than 3 messages are not erased: "Rohlik" is also 25 grocery orders.
- **Every store is built after erasure.** `deprecated/python/lab/results/brain_audit.json` finds none of the forgotten strings in the
  log, the facts, the 911 day gists, the 30 month summaries, or the statements.
- **A judgment call.** Erasing Marco's whole message also removed that he was the user's barber, which the request
  ("forget Marco's number, I don't go to that barber anymore") arguably covers.
- **Round 8 hardened this step after it leaked and over-erased on work data** (LLP 0014.000#forgetting). Jev now
  confirms every erasure, the LLM audits paraphrases in history, and the request is neutralized. The round-7 numbers
  above come from the earlier version.

## Caveats

- **One simulated person,** and I wrote the benchmark knowing the store design. The held-out questions were written
  after freezing, but by the same author.
- **The held-out set is small** (36; per-type numbers are 3 questions each).
- **The judge docks answers that leave out parenthetical detail of the gold.** It does so equally for every config.
- **Everything is answered by GPT-6 Luna at low effort.** A stronger model might count better in full context.
- **Latencies of cached replays are the originals.** Routing latencies come from the calls as first made.

## What this means

- **A memory for an AI should be a set of views over one log, not one kind of store.**
  - The base is facts + the raw log (rounds 2-4).
  - Add stores that compute what retrieval can't: aggregates, regularities and life periods.
  - The rest (episodes, intentions, preferences) are cheap indexes: $0.055 of the $0.13 build, plus a share of the
    Jev pass. They help when routing reads few stores, and change little when everything is read.
- **Route with a cheap decider tuned for recall.** Jev at 0.3 reads 3 stores in 0.3 s.
- **Consolidate with a cascade.** Jev asks cheap yes/no questions of everything (25k answers for $0.065), and the LLM
  extracts only from the 3% that pass.
- **Make erasure the first step of consolidation,** and audit every store.
- **Next** (LLP 0011):
  - event segmentation within days
  - decay of verbatim detail
  - real data (E6)
  - the agent loop (E4), where the stores would be tools

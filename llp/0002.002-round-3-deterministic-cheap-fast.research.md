## Question

Can FluidDB be deterministic, cheap and fast, with Jev replacing LLM calls, on a small budget?
- Reads should be as fast as possible.
- Writes may be slow.

[confirmed] (Adam Zvada, 2026-09-25, paraphrased from a dictated message): no large spend; make it deterministic,
cheap and high-performing; reads at the lowest latency, while writes may be slower and asynchronous. Details:
`deprecated/python/lab/REPORT.md` §20-22.

## Setup

- **Data:** the year database (v2.3, Luna low, after sleep) and its 60 questions at 12 months.
- **Grading:** Jev as judge, validated first against the Opus verdicts rebuilt from the cache for $0.
- **Budget:** ~$0.08 in total.

## Results

| finding | numbers | § |
|---|---|---|
| Jev as judge | 96.7% agreement with Opus on 300 verdicts, $0.006 | §20 |
| Deterministic reads (Jev fills a closed query form) | 77% of questions answered with no LLM in ~0.8 s for $0.0004, at 86% (73% by a strict rule grader) | §21 |
| Hybrid | 86.7% with a Sol fallback, 82.5% with Luna; the Luna agent alone 81.7% at 3.2 s | §21 |
| Deterministic forget propagation | 8 of 8 fully erased, nothing else lost, 5-20 ms, no model | §22 |
| Supersession | 4 of 4 real replacements, 1 debatable false positive, ~$0.0002 | §22 |
| Typed columns, compiled once | "best 10k time", "hardest grade" became exact | §22 |

## What we learned

- **Put the model at compile time or in the background,** not on the read path. Closed forms filled by Jev plus SQL
  are fast and cannot invent data.
- **Relaxation must be safe.** A "simpler" relaxation answered about the wrong person (LLP 0006#relaxation).
- **Completeness comes from structure, not from a smarter model.** A forget is complete when every value the erased
  rows held is gone.

## Decisions

- The deterministic reader with a verifier and an agent fallback (LLP 0006).
- Jev as the default judge (LLP 0009#graders).
- Forget propagation after every forget (LLP 0007).

## Caveats

- **The verifier threshold was chosen on the evaluated questions** (60).

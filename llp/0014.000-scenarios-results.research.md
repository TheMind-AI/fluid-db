## Summary

The same system ran with no domain code on three simulated work scenarios: a support desk (675 messages), a sales
pipeline (253) and an engineering team's notes (197). Everything was checked against exact truth, on 108 questions
written before any run.
- **It builds a sensible database for work data it has never seen.**
  - Support grew `tickets`, `organizations`, `responsibilities`, `communication_preferences`.
  - Sales grew `sales_leads`, `events`, `event_attendees`, `sales_territories`, `emails`.
  - Projects grew `tasks`, `incidents`, `teams`, `team_members`, `projects`.
  - Write accuracy: 96.8% (projects), 94.1% (sales), 82.6% (support).
- **Two write-path limits are specific to work data, and both are cheap to fix.**
  - **The Jev gate assumes a personal assistant.** It dropped 56 of 166 new support tickets: a customer asking for help
    looks like a question to the assistant. A domain-neutral wording writes 100% of fact-bearing messages, and stops
    70-80% of chit-chat instead of 98-100%.
  - **The write context can't see linked names.** The planner split 9 of 24 deals across two or three rows ("lost
    Humongous Insurance" didn't find the lead row, which only holds `organization_id`). Reading links as names (writer
    v2.4) leaves 3 of 24.
- **The compiled writer only learns from rows that are never updated.** It wrote 62% of the projects notes (1:1s and
  standups are events), 8% of sales and 0% of support: tickets and deals are updated by every later message.
- **Short histories need no memory system.**
  - The whole log in the prompt (5-21k tokens) scores 91.2% pooled, against 81.9% for the best memory (all seven
    stores).
  - Full context also wins on aggregates: 77.8% vs 66.7%. The facts inherit the write errors (the dropped tickets),
    and counting 30-40 items in context works.
- **The memory's gains over facts + log are small here.** All stores: +5.6 points pooled (95% CI 0.0 to +11.6), and
  ahead in all three scenarios.
  - The computed stores help only where the planner's schema lets them: demos on Tuesdays and Thursdays (58%/41%).
  - They can't answer "when are my 1:1s with Mei?", because the planner stored the person in the 1:1's title, not as a
    link.
- **Forgetting failed twice, in new ways, and is now checked twice.**
  1. The write path "cleared" a confidential note by update, and history kept a paraphrase of it. It leaked back in
     two configs.
  2. The erasure LLM then over-reached: it would have erased five unrelated chit-chat messages, and later the whole
     Fabrikam account.
  - Every whole-message and string erasure now needs Jev's confirmation.
  - Values the erased message touched are audited for paraphrases, and the forget request itself is neutralized.
  - Final state: no forgotten content appears in any answer.
- **The round cost about $2.45:** ingestion $0.80 fresh, $0.0007 per message.

## The three scenarios

Each is six months of one person's messages (Jan-Jun 2026), from `deprecated/python/lab/datasets/simulate_scenarios.py`, with the
truth of every fact-bearing message.

### A support desk

- **The data:** Maya Chen, support lead at Brightdesk, a scheduling SaaS.
  - Ticket notifications with the customer's words, her triage ("#1087 → Priya, P2"), resolutions, reopenings,
    escalations, satisfaction surveys and refunds.
  - Customers' contact preferences, a HIPAA rule, promises ("the CSV export fix by Mar 13"), and quarterly business
    reviews.
  - Team changes: Leo leaves, Ana takes billing, 24/7 coverage from May, a new SLA in June.
  - A GDPR erasure of one customer contact.
- **What FluidDB built:**
  - `tickets` (157 rows): number, category, title, description, received_at, the requester, organization, priority,
    status, resolution, CSAT, the assignee, escalation.
  - `organizations`, `people`, `responsibilities`, `communication_preferences`, `organization_policies`, `shifts`,
    `expenses` (the refunds), `events` (the reviews) and `tasks` (the promises).
- **Periods the memory found**, from the dated milestones alone:
  - Leo on the team until Apr 30
  - 24/7 coverage from May 1, Sam on nights
  - Ana owns billing from May 4
  - Enterprise first response within 4 hours from Jun 1
  - Acme contacted by email only
  - never paste patient data (HIPAA)
- **What worked:**
  - A ticket's history: opened, assigned, escalated, resolved, reopened, resolved again.
  - Contact preferences, the SLA, promises and their outcome.
  - The GDPR erasure: Carl Weber is gone from every store.
- **What didn't:**
  - A third of new tickets never got a row (the gate), so counts are wrong: "How many tickets did Acme open?"
  - "Who handles integration tickets?": the planner never stored a ticket's area as a column, so no routine can
    compute it.

### A sales pipeline

- **The data:** Daniel Park, account executive at Lumen Analytics.
  - 24 deals from lead to discovery, demo, proposal, negotiation (with discounts his manager approves by email) and
    won or lost to a competitor. Some close dates slip.
  - Weekly pipeline reviews, demos with a sales engineer, contacts' preferences, a territory change, a price
    increase.
  - A confidential note about a prospect's layoffs, which the user then asks to forget.
- **What FluidDB built:**
  - `sales_leads`: organization, contact, title, user count, status, proposed ARR, discount, approval, target close,
    proposal date.
  - `events` and `event_attendees` for calls and demos, `emails`, `sales_territories`, `sales_targets`,
    `communication_preferences`.
- **Periods:**
  - North America, then North America plus EMEA (Apr 1)
  - list price $1,500, then $1,800 per seat (May 1)
  - Wingtip's budget frozen from Mar 18
- **Routines:** demos happen on Tuesdays (58%) and Thursdays (41%), about one a week.
- **What worked:** the stage history of most deals, discounts and approvals, the pricing change and its 60-day grace,
  and erasure of the confidential note (after the fix, [Forgetting](#forgetting)).
- **What didn't:**
  - With writer v2.3, 9 of 24 deals were split across rows, so "which deals are in negotiation now?" found stale
    rows.
  - Two outcomes ("closed won: Fabrikam", "lost Wingtip") produced no write at all.

### A team's projects

- **The data:** Elena Rossi, engineering manager at Orbit Labs.
  - Biweekly 1:1s with five reports, standups, sprint planning and retros.
  - Decisions, one reversed after a load test.
  - Action items with owners and due dates, some slipped.
  - Three incidents with postmortems, a beta and a GA launch, a hire, and a reorganization into two teams.
  - A report's private health information, which the user asks to forget.
- **What FluidDB built:**
  - `tasks` (action items with assignee, due and status), `incidents` (duration, root cause, follow-ups).
  - `projects`, whose decisions are columns (`migration_strategy`; the reversal is in `_history`), and `teams` plus
    `team_members` for the reorganization.
  - `events` (122: 1:1s, standups, planning, retros), `hiring_plans`, `people`.
- **Periods:**
  - the Atlas decision (logical replication, Feb 10 to Mar 23; then dual writes)
  - Beacon's phases (built in React Native, beta from Apr 20, GA from Jun 15)
  - Jonas on the team from Apr 6
  - Platform and Apps teams from Jun 1
  - Jonas covering Comet while Tom was out
- **What worked:**
  - 96.8% of writes correct.
  - Decisions and their reversal.
  - Who owns what, before and after the reorganization.
  - Incidents and their causes.
  - Open and overdue action items (from the facts).
- **What didn't:**
  - Routines can't say when the 1:1s with Mei are: the person is in the event's name, not a link.
  - The intention store's status heuristics (built on personal reminders) missed a slipped deadline and misattributed
    an action item. When it disagreed with the `tasks` table, the answerer sometimes trusted it.

## Writes

Each scenario went through the round-4 tiered writer: a 120-message bootstrap through the planner, then compiled
patterns. `deprecated/python/lab/results/scenario_*_writes.json`.

| scenario | messages | truth records | write accuracy | compiled writer after bootstrap | skipped by the gate | cost if fresh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| projects | 197 | 93 | **96.8%** | 62% | 30 (chit-chat) | $0.08 |
| sales | 253 | 186 | **94.1%** (v2.4: 94.6%) | 8% | 43 | $0.17 |
| support | 675 | 614 | **82.6%** | 0% | 95, including 56 new tickets | $0.55 |

- **The gate.** Jev's write gate asks whether a message holds "a fact about the user's life … that a personal
  assistant should remember", and treats "asks the assistant a question" as skippable. A customer's ticket ("Hello
  team, we see this: can't update the credit card…") reads like a question.
  - The component test (`deprecated/python/lab/results/scenarios_gate.json`) ran the round-4 wording against a domain-neutral one:
    information "about people, organizations, work or life", and "a message from someone else is not a question to
    the assistant".

    | messages | round-4: facts written | round-4: chit-chat stopped | neutral: facts written | neutral: chit-chat stopped |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | support (636 with facts, 39 chat) | 91.2% | 100% | **100%** | 79% |
    | life, a sample (553, 47) | 100% | 98% | 100% | 70% |

  - The neutral wording loses nothing on the personal data. The chit-chat it lets through goes to the planner,
    which writes nothing, at ~$0.0007 each.
- **The compiled writer** learns from clean examples: a message that produced exactly one row, never touched again
  (LLP 0005). Tickets and deals are touched by every later message, so they never qualify. Tier A needs update
  patterns ("#(\d+) resolved: (.*)" → update the ticket with that number) to cover entity lifecycles.
- **Entity integrity: the split deals.** With writer v2.3, 34 lead rows held 24 deals, and 9 deals had two or three
  rows.
  - The planner sees the rows BM25 finds for the message. A lead row is `{"organization_id": 9, "status":
    "negotiating", …}`, so "lost Humongous Insurance to Quantix" matched the organization, not the lead, and a new
    lead was inserted.
  - Writer v2.4 (`deprecated/python/lab/systems/fluid_v2.py`, `link_ctx`) appends the linked rows' names to the text BM25 reads. The
    sales arm (`scenario_sales_v24`, $0.16 fresh): 27 lead rows, 3 split deals; write accuracy 94.6%.
  - Answers didn't change much: most questions didn't hinge on the split rows (`deprecated/python/lab/results/scenario_sales_v24_runs.json`).
- **The write check itself needed fixes.** These changed the measurement, not the system, and the messages stayed
  identical:
  - Stage words: "negotiating" for "negotiation", "closed" for "resolved".
  - Ticket numbers stored as integers.
  - Values a later message changed, found in the row's history.
  - A proposal recorded as `proposal_sent_date`.
  - The first reported numbers (5% support, 75% sales) were checker errors.

- **Full context wins, as the long-context results of round 2 predicted for histories that fit.**

  - Its misses are routines it would have to count ("when are my 1:1s with Mei?": 33% in projects) and a few

- **Variance is large at this size.** Re-running a scenario after a one-line change to its log (the neutralized
  forget request) moved full context by 5.5 points: re-sampled answers on 36 questions.

## Forgetting

Each scenario ends with one request to forget. The erasure step (LLP 0013.000#forgetting) failed in three ways that
the life data never showed. Each was fixed and re-run.

1. **A paraphrase in history.**
   - The write path handled "please forget what I noted about Fabrikam's layoffs" by updating the lead's
     `risk_notes` to "None".
   - The engine kept the old value in `_history`: "Confidentially reported by Hannah Weiss…: Fabrikam plans to lay
     off 15% of its operations staff in April".
   - The memory's erasure removed the original message but not this. Two configs told the user about the layoffs.
   - **Fix:** the LLM audits every value and history entry of the rows the erased message touched, and names those
     that repeat it in other words. Word-overlap rules missed this paraphrase.
2. **The request names what it forgets.** "Forget what I noted about Fabrikam's layoffs" itself says there were
   layoffs. **Fix:** once something is erased, the request becomes "[a request to forget something]".
3. **Over-erasure.**
   - With nothing left to find (the write path had already redacted the surgery message), the erasure LLM proposed
     five unrelated "remind me what we said about this later" messages.
   - Asked always to list identifying strings, it named "Fabrikam" and "Hannah Weiss" for the layoffs note. With a
     rule exempting strings that the request names, that erased the whole account.
   - Word-frequency rules failed both ways: they blocked "Carl Weber" (in dozens of tickets) and admitted "Fabrikam".
   - **Fix:** Jev, asked once per candidate, "Is this message/string itself what the request asks to forget?" It
     refused all five chit-chat messages, Carl's three ticket messages ("about Carl"? no: about tickets), "Fabrikam"
     and "Hannah Weiss". It confirmed the confidential note.
- **The simulator also had a bug.** It kept sending tickets from Carl Weber after his erasure, so "who is our contact
  at Initech?" had no fair gold. After the fix, Initech has a new contact from June, and support was re-ingested from
  June 11. The earlier messages replayed from cache.
- **Final state:** no answer in any config contains forgotten content (a scan of all 1,008 answers).
  - The remaining misses on forgotten questions are confabulations ("Priya Shah was the Initech contact"), not
    leaks.
  - "Why is Fabrikam stalling?" has a legitimate remaining answer (their on-prem requirement), so its UNKNOWN gold is
    arguably too strict.

## Hypotheses

Written before the simulators ran (LLP 0014#hypotheses).
- **H1 (write accuracy ≥ 85% in each scenario): 2 of 3.** Projects 96.8%, sales 94.1%, support 82.6%. The support
  misses are the gate's wording.
- **H2 (compiled writer ≥ 40% after the bootstrap): 1 of 3.** It holds on event streams, not on entity lifecycles.
- **H3 (`brain_all` ≥ `facts_log` + 5 pooled, CI excluding 0; ≥ in each): borderline.** +5.6 with CI 0.0 to +11.6;
  ahead in each.
- **H4 (routines + periods add ≥ 10 points on routine and period questions): not supported.** 66.7% vs 58.3% (+8.4 on
  18 questions). Routines depend on the planner storing people and categories as columns.
- **H5 (facts + log ≥ 80% on current and past state, each scenario): 2 of 3.** Support is 75%, because of the
  dropped tickets.
- **H6 (full context within 5 points of `brain_all`, below it on aggregates): refuted, the other way.** Full context
  is 9.3 points above the memory and above it on aggregates too (77.8% vs 66.7%).

## Deviations

- **The memory was generalized before the scenarios ran:** Jev aspects reworded, periods from milestones, routines
  split by period and person, intentions with owners.
  - Control on the life data: `brain_all` 91.5% dev (round 7: 94.1%), 91.7% held-out (round 7: 91.7%);
    `brain_jev_recall` 94.1% and 88.9% (round 7: 93.1% and 88.9%).
  - Results: `deprecated/python/lab/results/brain_runs_v2.json`, `brain_runs_holdout_v2.json`.
- **Write-check fixes.** Measurement only, listed in [Writes](#writes).
- **The support simulator's post-erasure contact** ([Forgetting](#forgetting)).
- **Erasure fixed three times after scenario results** ([Forgetting](#forgetting)). The final code re-ran every
  scenario's memory. The pre-fix results are kept:
  - `scenario_sales_runs_prefix.json`: two configs leaked.
  - `scenario_projects_runs_prefix.json`
  - `scenario_support_runs_v1.json`: before the simulator fix.
- **Two arms added:** writer v2.4 on sales, and the gate wording test.
- **Support's first ingestion was restarted** before it would have hit its $0.60 guard, and replayed from the cache.

## What this means

- **As a self-building ETL, FluidDB generalizes to work data** (94-97% of writes correct), once two things are fixed:
  1. **Gate wording for any domain.** Ship the neutral wording; the cost is a few extra planner calls on chit-chat.
  2. **Link-aware write context** (v2.4), so an update finds the entity row it belongs to.
- **The compiled writer needs update patterns** before it can take over stateful entities: tickets, deals, orders.
- **For question answering over histories that fit in context, read everything.**
  - The memory system earns its keep at scale: round 7's 5,050 messages (122k tokens), where full context lost by 13
    points.
  - It also earns it for structure: SQL, the generated app, history, and verifiable forgetting.
- **Computed stores are only as good as the schema under them.** A routine can be computed per person only if the
  planner linked the person.
  - Next step: split routines by any column that repeats (the 1:1 title), not only by links.
- **Erasure needs a second opinion on every deletion,** and an audit of what the deleted message changed. LLM
  proposals alone both leak and over-erase.

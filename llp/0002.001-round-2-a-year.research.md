## Question

- Does FluidDB hold up over time?
- How does it compare with Mem0, the most-used memory library, and on public memory benchmarks?
- Can forgetting be made complete?

## Setup

- **The year:** `simulate_year.py`, 817 messages from October 2025 to September 2026. It includes:
  - a move from Prague to San Francisco
  - changing numbers and jobs
  - documents
  - two forget requests
  - exact truth, with 30 questions at 6 months and 60 at 12 months
- **Writers:** v2.2 and v2.3 with GPT-6 Luna (reasoning off or low) and GPT-6 Sol.
- **Readers:** the raw log, the database dump, dump + log, and tool agents (Luna, Sol).
- **Baselines:** Mem0 2.2 OSS on the same year; LoCoMo under Mem0's protocol; LongMemEval_S with its original
  protocol (101-question sample).

## What we learned

- **Spend on the reader, not the writer.** Once the structure is good, answer quality depends on the reader.
- **Structure wins on aggregates and state.** Fact lists win on conversational detail. Keep both: the raw log is part
  of FluidDB.
- **Both public benchmarks fit in a context window,** so they no longer measure memory. Evaluating memory needs data
  larger than context, or agent tasks (LLP 0011).
- **Forgetting needs verification.** The planner's forgets leave copies in history and the log.

## Decisions

- Planner v2.3 rules (LLP 0005#the-planner).
- `forget` with redaction, then a verification pass (LLP 0007#forget-propagation, round 3).

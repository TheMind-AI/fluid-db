# LLP 0017.000: Real conversations: results

**Type:** Research
**Status:** Draft

**Author:** Adam Zvada / Claude

**Related:** LLP 0017, LLP 0015, LLP 0016.000, LLP 0013.000, LLP 0004

## Summary

The first round on real data: one person's 164 chats with a therapy assistant (July 2024 to September 2026), and
that product's own memory of them. Everything real stayed in a private, git-ignored directory. This document reports
counts, rates and kinds of information only (LLP 0017#consent-and-data-handling).
- **People re-tell a lot.** About a third of the lasting things the person said in a session had already been said
  in earlier sessions: 69 of 208 statements (33%), and 38% after a 14-month break. Most were how they want to be
  talked to, their work, and the practices they use.
- **No memory's briefing at the start of a chat would have spared the re-telling.** Every memory's 250-word
  briefing (they wrote 48-76 words) held at most a tenth of what the person went on to re-tell. Reading the whole
  history did no better. A summary keeps the themes; what gets re-told is detail.
- **Asked on cue** ("what have I told you about X?"), the memories built before each session knew:
  - what the product injects into its prompt: 37%
  - everything the product stores, searchable: 38%
  - FluidDB: 49-54%, +16 points over the product (95% CI 6 to 27)
  - the whole history read at once (~13-75k tokens): 66%, +12 over FluidDB (CI 3 to 22)
- **FluidDB's gap to reading everything** is mostly on facts first said in Czech: 27% against 60% in English, on 13
  statements. Its log is searched by words, across a bilingual history.
- **The write path turned therapy talk into a mood diary:** 158 of 176 rows at the end are mood entries, and people
  are almost absent. The planner was shaped on logistics-like personal data.
- **A request to forget the person's own first name deleted 54 rows:** the planner forgot the user's own row, and the
  engine's forget cascaded into every row linked to it. Forget can now redact links instead (`forget_links="redact"`,
  writer v25r); a replay keeps all 54.
- **The product's memory shows only recent material to its assistant.** For this person, what the prompt gets today
  comes from the last two weeks; the two earlier years are invisible to it.
- **The round cost about $2.37:** $2.27 of Luna, estimated from logged tokens, and $0.10 of Jev.

## Data

- 164 chats and 4,141 messages: 1,876 from the person, 2,236 from the assistant, 29 tool results. The person's
  messages are short (median 30 characters) and mix Czech and English.
- 385 windows of up to 6 of the person's messages, each with the assistant's preceding question; 317k characters.
- The export, the datasets, the databases, the answers and the gold are in the private directory
  (LLP 0017#consent-and-data-handling).

## Part A: the whole history

### Writes on real conversation

`scenarios ingest own@v25nog`; `real_return schema own@v25nog`.

| | |
|---|---|
| windows | 385 |
| skipped by the gate | 112 (29%) |
| written by the planner | 273 (71%), 336 operations |
| written by compiled code | 0: two compiles proposed 38 patterns for mood entries and kept none |
| cost | $0.43 |
| tables | 8: mood entries, interests, software issues, projects, workouts, people, career plans, possessions |
| rows at the end | 176, of which 158 mood entries (4 with a numeric rating, 87 with a named stressor) |
| rows before the 2026 chats | 213: 146 mood entries, 51 interests, 3 career plans, 2 people |

- **Therapy talk became a mood diary.** The planner, shaped on messages that carry transactions and events (LLP
  0005), wrote nearly every window as a mood entry. It kept the person's relationships, plans and practices only in
  the notes of those entries, or not at all.
- **The product recorded a 1-5 mood rating in 34 of its 105 session notes.** Neither memory tracks mood as a series.
- **No pattern was compiled.** Real conversation doesn't repeat templates, as H6 expected.

### The forget that deleted the user

- **In September 2026 the person asked the assistant to forget their first name,** testing the product's memory
  tools. FluidDB's planner turned this into `forget` of `people` row 1, the user's own row.
- **The engine's forget cascades into rows that link to the forgotten one** (LLP 0004#forget). That deleted all 51
  interests and all 3 career plans, the rows about the user.
- **The background erasure pass saw 3 forget requests and erased nothing more:** Jev refused both whole-message
  proposals.
- **The fix is an engine option:** `forget_links="redact"` nulls the link and keeps the linked rows (writer v25r,
  `deprecated/python/lab/systems/engine.py`). A replay of the 36 windows after the break, from the cache, kept the 51 interests and 3
  career plans. The second forget request in those chats removed only the one item it named. The replay cost $0.02.
- **Two lessons for the planner** [inferred]:
  - A request to forget an attribute (a name, a detail) should clear that value, not forget the row.
  - The subject of the memory, the user's own row, should never be forgotten by one message.

### The 12 probes, read

`scenarios memory own@v25nog`. There is no gold; this is a reading of 72 answers. Evidence per question: 4k
characters (what the product injects), 17-29k (the product's full memory, FluidDB) and 325k (the whole history).
- **What the product injects today answers from the last two weeks only.**
  - It placed the start of the person's conversations in September 2026, two years late.
  - It knew one person in their life.
  - When a profile exists, the product's prompt builder no longer reads the older topic summaries and session
    notes. The profile is rewritten when chats change it; for this person it and all 14 memories reflect
    September 2026 [inferred mechanism].
- **The product's full memory, searched,** dates the start correctly from its 2024 session notes. Its answers are
  still led by the recent memories.
- **FluidDB spans the whole history, with dates.**
  - **Follow-up is its clearest win.** Its brain configs listed 8-10 plans that were never followed up, each with the
    date it was made and its status, from 2024 onwards. The product's memories named one plan, from September 2026.
  - It gave the work history as a dated trajectory.
  - It was weak on people (one left after the cascade), on when the conversations started, and on preferences. There
    it reported a wish stated once, which contradicts a complaint the person repeated across many sessions.
- **Reading the whole history gave the most complete answers** on most probes: people, practices, preferences, the
  start and the recent sessions. It answered "I don't know" on what helped most.

- **"Lasting" is a Jev score of at least 0.2** for "a lasting fact about the person". The 44 statements below it were
  moods and events of the moment, and product feedback. Counting all statements, 80 of 252 (32%) were re-told.
- **What is re-told most:**
  - how the person wants to be talked to: 27 of 64 (42%)
  - practices and techniques: 11 of 26 (42%)
  - work: 15 of 38 (39%)
  - feelings: 6 of 27 (22%)
  - plans: 4 of 19 (21%)
  - life: 4 of 18 (22%)
  - health: 2 of 13 (15%)
- **How the gold was found.** The reader of the whole history named the earlier windows for 74 of the 80 re-told
  statements. BM25 found the other 6, and every match was verified on its own window.

### Briefings at the start of a chat

The pre-registered test. Recall of the 69 lasting re-told statements, per briefing:

| memory, built at the cut | briefing on the chat's first message | generic briefing |
|---|---|---|
| what the product injects | 2.9% | 8.7% |
| the product's full memory, searched | 2.9% | 4.3% |
| FluidDB facts + log | 5.8% | 2.9% |
| FluidDB all stores | 0.0% | 1.4% |
| FluidDB, Jev routing | 5.8% | 2.9% |
| the whole history | 7.2% | 10.1% |

- **The new statements are the control.** Every briefing held at most 1.6% of them.
- **The judge was reworded once.** The first wording marked every statement as missing, even plain paraphrases.
  The second agrees with hand labels on 38 of 40 sampled pairs: it found 2 of the 4 hand-labelled hits, with no
  false positives.
- **Why so low.** The briefings average 48-76 words, and a summary keeps themes: work stress, focus, the language
  the person speaks. What people re-tell is detail: a technique that didn't help, the exact kind of question they
  dislike. Most chats also open with a greeting, so the first message gives the memory little to aim at.

### Recall on cue

LLP 0017#amendment-recall-on-cue. One question per re-told or changed statement, answered by each memory built at
the cut and graded by the lab's Jev judge (partial = 0.5).

| memory, built at the cut | re-told (69) | changed: the earlier version (13) |
|---|---|---|
| what the product injects | 37.0% | 34.6% |
| the product's full memory, searched | 37.7% | 42.3% |
| FluidDB facts + log | 49.3% | 57.7% |
| FluidDB all stores | 50.0% | 65.4% |
| FluidDB, Jev routing | **53.6%** | 50.0% |
| the whole history | **65.9%** | 57.7% |

- **Paired differences on the re-told questions:** FluidDB with Jev routing, minus each other memory, with 95% CI.
  - minus what the product injects: +16.7 (6.5 to 26.8)
  - minus the product's full memory: +15.9 (5.1 to 26.8)
  - minus FluidDB facts + log: +4.3 (-5.1 to 13.8)
  - minus the whole history: -12.3 (-21.7 to -2.9)
- **Per cut:** the same order at every cut, with one exception.

  | cut | product injects | product, searched | FluidDB, Jev routing | whole history |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | August 2024 | 43.2% | 38.6% | 52.3% | 56.8% |
  | September-November 2024 | 41.2% | 33.8% | 57.4% | 72.1% |
  | the return | 15.4% | 46.2% | 46.2% | 65.4% |

  - The exception: at the return, what the product injects knew 15%. Its older memory held little of what came up
    in 2026.
- **By the language of the earlier window** (the words the fact was first said in):

  | memory | English (56) | Czech (13) |
  |---|---|---|
  | FluidDB, Jev routing | 60% | 27% |
  | FluidDB all stores | 49% | 54% |
  | the whole history | 66% | 65% |

  - FluidDB's routed read leans on word search over the log, which misses across languages. Its derived stores are
    written in English and bridge the gap.
  - 13 statements is a small sample.
- **The controls didn't work as a test of hallucination.** A question about a new statement names a topic, and the
  history often covers that topic without the new fact. A good answer then reports what is known, and the grader
  counts it against "never mentioned". In 7 answers read by hand, none invented a fact.

### The assistant's questions

- **The automated check failed.** It flagged 204 of the assistant's 1,117 questions as already answered by the
  history. Most were check-ins about the present ("any updates since last time?").
- **By hand:** 5-7 of 20 flagged questions really asked for something the history held, such as which tasks feel
  overwhelming. That puts re-asking at roughly 1 in 20 of the assistant's questions, on a weak measure.

## What the product's memory teaches

The product's design, at the level needed here:
- typed memories, added and never edited
- a profile of a few blocks, rewritten when a chat changes them
- a note per chat
- recall by word overlap, as a tool the assistant can call
- older generations: topic summaries and a note per session, with insights, action steps and some mood ratings
  [observed] the export's shape and the product's source

What this round shows:
- **Keeping memories is not the problem; showing them is.** The product stored the person's history, and its
  searched memory knew about as much as its injected one. But what reaches the prompt is chosen by recency: the
  newest memories and a profile that the recent chats rewrote.
  - On recall on cue, it lags the memories that search the whole history by 16 points.
  - At the 14-month return it knew 15%.
- **A profile rewritten from recent chats forgets by drift** [inferred]. Nothing was deleted, yet the earlier years
  were gone from what the assistant sees.
- **Word-overlap recall has FluidDB's weakness,** across the two languages this person speaks.
- **Plans have no status.** Follow-up is left to the prompt; FluidDB's intention store, with a status per plan, gave
  the clearest follow-up answer.
- **Mood was rated in a third of the sessions, and never read back** as a series.
- **Therapy memory holds sensitive history.** What a memory volunteers unprompted needs a policy, in the briefing and
  in the answer, not only in the opening line [inferred].

## Hypotheses

From LLP 0017#hypotheses and its amendments; pooled over the three cuts, on lasting statements.
- **H1 (≥ 25% re-told): supported.** 33% (per cut 27%, 37%, 38%).
- **H2 (what the product injects holds ≤ 40% in its briefing): supported,** trivially. It held 3-9%, and no memory
  did much better.
- **H3 (FluidDB's briefing ≥ 60%, +20 over the product): refuted.** 3-6%.
- **H4 (reading everything within 5 points of FluidDB, or better, on briefings): supported.** 7-10%.
- **H5 (≥ 20% of the assistant's questions already answered): not supported,** on a weak measure: about 5%.
- **H6 (writes on real conversation): supported.**
  - The gate wrote 71% of windows; compiled code wrote 0%.
  - Ingestion cost $0.43.
  - The schema grew 8 tables, among them people and moods, though people are nearly empty.
- **H7 (whole-history probes): half.** FluidDB won on follow-up and the dated work history. It was not better on the
  start, the recent sessions or preferences. The whole history was best overall.
- **H8 (recall on cue): half.**
  - FluidDB answered 49-54%.
  - The product's searched memory answered 38%, under 50%.
  - What the product injects answered 37%, over the predicted 30%.
- **H9 (reading everything ≥ FluidDB - 5): supported.** +12.3.
- **H10 (the controls): not a valid test,** for the reason above.

## What this changes in the design (LLP 0015)

- **Real conversation carries statements, not rows** [inferred]. What a person tells a therapist are lasting
  statements about themselves: preferences, practices, struggles, plans and people. The planner's tables caught few
  of them.
  - The statements this round's gold extracted (third person, one fact, a kind, with provenance) are the natural
    unit.
  - That is also the product's memory model.
  - FluidDB's intention and preference stores are close to it; they need recurrence counts and status.
- **Memory must act on cue, not only at the start.**
  - A briefing at the start of a chat holds a tenth of what gets re-told.
  - The memory has to be queried as topics come up, and find the detail.
  - The lab's configs answer one question at a time; the next test is per turn, inside a conversation.
- **When one person's history fits, reading it all recalls the most** (66% at 13-75k tokens), consistent with LLP
  0016.000's read policy.
  - It costs about $0.008 per read with Luna. Per turn, that adds up.
  - A tested middle way is still missing: a nightly read of everything into a dossier of statements with counts and
    dates, searched per turn.
- **Retrieval must cross languages.** Word search over a bilingual log lost more than half the Czech-sourced facts.
  - Derived stores written in one language, or a Jev relevance pass, avoided it.
- **Forget:**
  - An attribute clears a value.
  - The user's own row is never forgotten by one message.
  - Links are redacted at write time, and removal is left to the verified background pass (LLP 0004#forget).

## Deviations

- **The protocol changed three times after the first results,** each before the numbers it affects were scored.
  1. Two cuts were added, because the 2026 return was mostly product testing.
  2. A lasting-fact filter was added, with its threshold chosen from the statements.
  3. The recall-on-cue test was added, after the briefings scored near zero for every memory.
- **The briefing judge was reworded once,** after it marked everything as missing, and was then checked against 40
  hand labels.
- **The assistant's-questions check was reworded once** and then abandoned for a hand count of 20.
- **The spend passed the RFC's $2** because of the added test: $2.37 for the round.
- **One person, 58 sessions, 69 re-told statements.** Differences under about 10 points are within noise. The
  person is also the product's developer, and some sessions are tests.

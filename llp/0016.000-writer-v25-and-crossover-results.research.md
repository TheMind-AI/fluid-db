# LLP 0016.000: Writer v2.5, and when a memory beats reading everything: results

**Type:** Research
**Status:** Draft
**Systems:** Writes, Reads, Research
**Author:** Adam Zvada / Claude
**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Related:** LLP 0016, LLP 0015, LLP 0014.000, LLP 0013.000, LLP 0005

## Summary

- **Writer v2.5 fixes the support desk's writes, but not yet its answers.**
  - The neutral gate skipped none of the 166 new tickets (v2.3: 56).
  - Compiled update patterns wrote 203 ticket updates in code, 37% of the messages after the bootstrap (v2.3: 0%), and
    92% of them were right.
  - Write accuracy is 88.6%, or 97.3% leaving out 19 tickets that the GDPR erasure deleted as part of the contact's
    data (v2.3: 82.3% and 88.3%). Ingestion costs 20% less.
  - Answers barely moved (facts + log 75.0 → 76.4). The remaining errors are the reader's on a new schema: a count
    over the wrong filter, and a company stored as a link in some rows and as text in others.
- **There is no single crossover length.**
  - On one person's life, the memory is flat across 26k, 51k and 122k tokens of history: 88.9-93.1% with Jev
    routing. Full context scores 83.3%, 90.3% and 83.3%.
  - Full context doesn't get worse with length on questions about the recent months. It fails the same questions at
    every length: counting 42 climbs, summing 191 coffees, which book was open on a given day, recalling every open
    reminder.
  - On round 8's work scenarios, which have few countable items, full context won instead.
  - What decides is how much a question must count, sum or track, not how many tokens the history has.
- **The whole log plus the database's computed answer** (a new config) scored between the two at 6 months (86.1%).
  It helps only when the computed answer itself is right.
- **Two engine findings:**
  - Erasing a person cascades to rows that link to them. Carl Weber's 19 support tickets went with him; the business
    records could have stayed with his name redacted.
  - Links the engine's naming rule can't resolve (`assignee_id` → people) show as bare ids in the readable view.
- **The round cost about $2.25.**

## Part A: writer v2.5 on the support desk

`deprecated/python/lab/results/scenario_support_v25_writes.json`, `scenario_support_v25_runs.json`. v2.5 = v2.4 (link-aware write
context) + the neutral gate + compiled updates (`deprecated/python/lab/systems/compiled_writer.py`: `examples_from_ops`,
`compile_updates`, `_find`).

| | v2.3 (round 8) | v2.5 |
|---|---|---|
| new tickets skipped by the gate | 56 of 166 | **0** |
| messages written by compiled code after the bootstrap | 0% | **37%** (203 updates, 4 inserts) |
| compiled writes that are right | – | 92.1% |
| write accuracy | 82.3% | **88.6%** |
| write accuracy without the 19 erased tickets | 88.3% | **97.3%** |
| ingestion cost, fresh | $0.55 | $0.44 |
| answers: facts + log / all stores / full context | 75.0 / 77.8 / 90.3 | 76.4 / 77.8 / 90.3 |

- **Update patterns work.** The compile at message 120 kept 6 validated patterns from 66 examples. An example:
  "#1087 → Priya, P2" finds the ticket by `ticket_number` and sets the assignee (by name, through the link) and the
  priority.
- **Insert patterns for new tickets didn't.** The LLM copied the prompt's example framing ("MESSAGE (sent …):") into
  its regexes, and every pattern failed validation. The examples should be shown as bare text. This is a fix for the
  next round.
- **The write check needed more fixes.** A survey linked to a ticket holds the ticket's number through the link. A
  link the naming rule can't resolve falls back to the linked row's name. These were measurement fixes only. Round
  8's support number moves from 82.6% to 82.3%; sales and projects are unchanged.
- **Why the answers didn't follow.**
  - "How many tickets did Acme open?": the reader counted 93 (truth 37).
  - "How many were escalated?": it counted all 151. The column is `escalation_target`, and the plan didn't filter on
    it.
  - 32 tickets carry the company only as text (`organization_name`), not as the link.
  - The next gains are on the read side: plans on new schemas, and one link column per entity.

## Part B: the crossover

The same 36 questions about April-September 2026, at three history lengths (`deprecated/python/lab/datasets/crossover.py`). The 6- and
12-month databases were ingested from their window alone (v2.3, $0.21 and $0.39); the 30-month one is round 4's.
`deprecated/python/lab/results/crossover_summary.json`.

| history | tokens | full context | Jev router (0.3) | all stores | facts + log | full context + facts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 months | 26k | 83.3 | 90.3 | 90.3 | 81.9 | 86.1 |
| 12 months | 51k | 90.3 | **93.1** | 88.9 | 77.8 | – |
| 30 months | 122k | 83.3 | 88.9 | 87.5 | 81.9 | – |

- **Jev router minus full context:** +6.9 (95% CI -2.8 to +18.1), +2.8 (-4.2 to +11.1), +5.6 (-2.8 to +15.3). Ahead
  at every length, but never significant on 36 questions.
- **Full context's misses are the same at every length:**
  - **Counting and summing:** 40 or 43 climbs for 42; no coffee total at all. Aggregates: 33% at 26k and at 122k.
  - **Intervals:** "which book was I reading when Eva moved?" → "I don't know".
  - **Recall of lists:** one of two open reminders; three of four 5/5 movies.
- **Length alone doesn't hurt it here.** The extra 24 months of older messages didn't lower the score on these
  recent-months questions. The spread (83-90%) is sampling noise; one changed line moved a score by 5 points in round
  8.
- **Why round 8 differed.**
  - The work scenarios have few countable items (9 refunds, 3 incidents, 24 deals), which a model can count in
    context.
  - Their facts had write errors (a third of the support tickets missing).
  - This life has 191 coffees, 42 climbs and 166 sleep logs in six months.
- **The hybrid** (the whole log plus the facts store's computed answer and matching rows) scored 86.1% at 6 months.
  Its coffee total was still wrong, because the computed answer ignored "since moving": the plan had no date filter.

## Hypotheses

Written before the runs (LLP 0016#hypotheses).
- **H1 (v2.5 write accuracy ≥ 92%, no new ticket skipped): mostly.**
  - No ticket skipped.
  - 97.3% on the tickets that exist; 88.6% counting the 19 that the erasure deleted.
- **H2 (compiled code ≥ 40% of messages, ≥ 95% right, ≥ 30% cheaper): not met.** 37%, 92.1%, 20%. Insert patterns
  for tickets failed on a prompt-format error.
- **H3 (facts + log answers +5): not supported.** +1.4. The remaining errors are the reader's.
- **H4 (a crossover between 26k and 122k tokens): refuted.**
  - The memory is ahead at every length on this data.
  - Full context won at 5-21k tokens in round 8, on sparse data. The crossover is a property of the questions and the
    data, not of the length.
- **H5 (the memory flat, full context dropping ≥ 10): half.** The memory is flat (88.9-93.1). Full context doesn't
  drop.

## What this changes in the design (LLP 0015)

- **The read policy is not "read everything when it fits".**
  - Questions that count, sum, compare over time or track state go to the facts, at any length.
  - Questions about what was said, and short sparse histories, can be read whole.
  - Routing by the kind of question, which Jev already does per store, is the right shape. A router rule "aggregate
    → facts even when the log fits" is the next thing to test.
- **Better writes need better reads.** Once writes are right, the misses move to the reader's plans on schemas it
  hasn't seen. The planner also stored the same relation two ways (link and text).
- **Erasure of a person should redact, not cascade,** into records that belong to someone else (LLP 0004#forget).
- **The compiled writer's prompts must show the examples bare,** so the LLM doesn't pattern-match the framing.

## Deviations

- **Two crossover questions were reworded** before any run ("who do I usually climb with *these days*", "a coffee *in
  San Francisco*"). Over 30 months the unqualified versions have a different answer (Tom, and CZK).
- **The write check was fixed twice more** (links; unresolvable link names). All scenarios were re-scored, with the
  messages and systems unchanged.
- **Added one config after the 6-month results:** `full_facts`. It ran at 6 months only, to stay within budget.

## Summary

On the round-5 database (5,050 messages, 4,516 rows checked against the truth, 163 of them wrong):
- **The database knows what it doubts.** Code checks plus a generic Jev check separate wrong rows from right ones
  with AUROC 0.988.
  - Reviewing the 5% least-confident rows catches 80% of the wrong ones.
  - This cost $0.049 for 4,571 rows.
- **The inbox fixes the data.** A user who answers the top 100 items fixes 71 wrong rows: 3.6% → 2.0%.
  - A random 100 fixes 4.
  - Code suggests a one-click fix for 99 of the 100 items, and every suggested value is right.
- **The app builds itself from the query log and the schema.**
  - 16 tiles, people cards, breakdowns, a timeline and the inbox, over 5 pages.
  - Every tile's SQL agrees with the numbers shown. Layout cost $0.0006. Computing the views takes 0.3 s.
  - It answers 73.75% of future questions of logged shapes by reading a tile (75.0% after the inbox), and 44% of
    novel shapes.
- **Generality was partial.** On the year database, only 5 tiles came from its 60 questions, and 43% of its
  aggregation questions were read off correctly. Every tile answer equals the reader's own.
- **The round cost about $0.12.**

## Confidence

163 of the 4,516 rows that truth messages produced are wrong (3.6%): 123 dates, 52 companions, 2 grades, 1 amount,
1 merchant. `deprecated/python/lab/results/app_calibration.json`.

| strategy (who gets reviewed first) | AUROC | wrong rows caught when reviewing 1% | 2% | 5% | 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | 0.50 | 1% | 2% | 6% | 11% |
| the planner's rows first | 0.55 | 0% | 1% | 5% | 10% |
| code signals | 0.974 | 21% | 37% | 75% | 97% |
| Jev alone (generic, untuned) | 0.803 | 16% | 31% | 46% | 53% |
| **combined** | **0.988** | 16% | 40% | **80%** | **99%** |

- **Code signals are precise where they know the error.**
  - A late-night date is always wrong (precision 100%) and finds 65% of the wrong rows.
  - A missing date is always wrong too, but rare.
  - The people checks are noisier (about 40% precise), because a message can name a person the row rightly
    doesn't link.
- **Jev is strong on people and weak on dates.** At 5% it catches 96% of the wrong companions but 31% of the wrong
  dates.
  - It can't know the late-night convention; a code rule can.
  - Its three questions alone reach AUROC 0.67 (faithful), 0.58 (date) and 0.75 (people). Their minimum is 0.80.
- **The planner's rows aren't where the errors are.** Reviewing them first is no better than random.

## Inbox

An oracle user answers the top N items of each strategy truthfully. Each answer is logged as a message and applied
as a typed update. `deprecated/python/lab/results/app_inbox.json`.

| wrong rows left of 163, after answering | 25 | 50 | 100 | 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | 163 | 161 | 159 | 155 |
| Jev | 145 | 136 | 112 | 92 |
| code | 141 | 124 | 101 | 58 |
| **combined** | 144 | 134 | **92** | **44** |

- **Suggested fixes, by code** (`confidence.suggest`, `deprecated/python/lab/results/app_suggestions.json`):
  - a late-night date becomes the evening before
  - a person who isn't linked, or is linked wrongly, becomes the one the message names; an exact stored name
    beats a first-name match
  - Of the top 100 combined items, 99 get a suggestion and every suggested value is right (dates 43/43, people
    85/85). For 71 of the 74 wrong rows, applying the suggestion fixes every wrong field, so the user confirms
    instead of typing.
- **Person questions.** The dedupe pass left three pairs at 0.58-0.72: Dave/David, David/David Mokos and Dave/David
  Mokos. The oracle merged them into David Mokos.
- **Answers barely moved.** The deterministic reader on the corrected database scores 61.9%, unchanged. Its misses
  are routing and wording, not the fixed values. The app's tiles move a little: see [App](#app).

## App

`deprecated/python/lab/appgen.py`, `deprecated/python/lab/results/app_build.json`, screenshots in `deprecated/python/lab/app/*.png`.

**What was generated:**
- **16 tiles from 70 trusted plans.** Measures: spend, counts and averages of expenses; the most expensive
  purchase; averages and minima of body measurements; runs; climbing sessions. Slicers: category, dining
  occasion, merchant, location.
- **One card per person,** with everything linked to them.
- **The most frequent merchants,** per year and per category.
- **Category × location totals.**
- **The timeline** and **the inbox.**

The layout call put them on pages: Spending, Health & activity, People, Timeline & checks, and More (the one tile
the layout forgot). Every tile carries "why this is here": the questions that produced it, and its SQL.

**Checks:**
- **Every tile's SQL agrees with the app's own numbers** (0 mismatches). A group whose rows have no amount sums to
  NULL in SQL and shows no cell in the app; that counts as agreement.
- **Cost and speed:** layout $0.0006; the views compute in 0.2-0.3 s and render in 0.1 s. The first page is 144 KB;
  the timeline page, with every dated row, is 1.1 MB.

**Reading the 105 future questions off the app** (oracle navigation: knowing which view to open):

| | all | logged shapes (80) | same | reworded | novel (25) | no view | view shows a wrong number |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before the inbox | 66.7% | **73.75%** | 77.5% | 70.0% | 44% | 12 | 23 |
| after answering 100 | 68.6% | 75.0% | 80.0% | 70.0% | 48% | 10 | 23 |
| after answering 200 | 70.5% | 77.5% | 82.5% | 72.5% | 48% | 10 | 21 |

**Exact for:**
- spend and visits per merchant
- runs
- lowest weight
- average sleep
- the most frequent merchant per kind and year (a novel shape, from the generic breakdown)
- 80% of "spend with a person" (novel, from the person cards)

**The misses are the data's and the schema's, not the app's:**
- **No view.** Round 5's location column doesn't cover dining, so "priciest dinner in San Francisco" has nowhere to
  be read.
- **Wrong numbers:**
  - late-night dates that cross a month
  - a planner-added `title = 'Climbing'` filter that drops a few climbs
  - Rohlík deliveries counted as groceries

## Generality

`deprecated/python/lab/results/app_year.json`. The same generator ran on the year database of rounds 2-3, with its own 60 questions:
- **5 tiles came from 33 trusted plans.** A 60-question log rarely asks the same shape twice.
- **Every tile's SQL agrees.**
- **7 of its 14 aggregation questions** can be read off a tile, and 6 of those are right: **42.9%**.
- **Every tile answer equals the deterministic reader's answer** for that question (1,025 Kč of coffee in November,
  $19,200 of rent…). The seventh is a data error (Blue Bottle: 143.50 vs 148.75).
- **The other 7** had plans the verifier didn't trust, or shapes that never repeated (a trip's flights plus hotel).

**What the year database taught:**
- **Tiles must use the reader's matching rules.** That schema stores kinds in two overlapping columns (`category`
  and `item`). The reader treats the same value picked for both as one concept, matched in either column. Tiles
  first required both, and got 620 Kč where the reader got 1,025. Concept keys (`category|item`, and a UNION in the
  SQL) fixed it.
- **The reader listed the names it found in a question in a nondeterministic order,** so cached requests missed
  between processes. They are sorted now.

## What had to be learned

Each of these came from looking at the app or at the data, and was fixed with a general rule.
1. **Let the number of series choose the chart.** Eight merchants as lines were unreadable. More than 4 series
   become ranked bars (top 10), with a per-year table (top 15, the rest collapsed).
2. **One axis per unit.** Weight (kg) and sleep (hours) on one axis flattened sleep. Series whose scales differ by
   more than 3× get a panel each.
3. **Levels zoom; totals keep zero.** Averages and minima use a zoomed axis; sums and counts keep the zero baseline.
4. **Views that repeat or are empty go.** Two tiles with identical numbers are merged; empty breakdowns are dropped.
5. **Show fields, highlight the doubt, suggest the fix.** Raw JSON in the inbox became a field list, with the
   suspicious fields highlighted and a one-click suggestion.
6. **An exact name beats a first name.** "with Eva" was first suggested as Eva Green, a colleague.
7. **Tiles must match the way the reader matches,** and **deterministic requests keep caches valid** (see
   [Generality](#generality)).

## Deviations

- **The rendering changed after screenshots** (items 1-5). These changes affect how the app looks, not the numbers
  measured.
- **Concept keys and the NULL-sum rule were added after the year test's first run.** The first run scored 28.6%, and
  the SQL check failed.
- **The reader's name ordering was fixed during the round.** Earlier cached runs replay; runs with named people make
  new requests.
- **The code signals and the suggestion rules come from round 4's error analysis of this same data.** They are
  in-sample. Jev (H2) is the untuned baseline.

## Limitations

- **The oracle user is perfect.** Real users skip items and make mistakes.
- **The app lookup knows which view to open.** Whether a person finds the number is for the user to judge.
- **A single simulated person and domain.** The year database shows that a short log yields few tiles.
- **The static app records inbox answers in the browser only.** Writing them back needs a small server (E1 follow-up).

## Summary

Round 10's test, on 18 other people who used the therapy assistant for their own lives. Their conversations went to
OpenAI only, and only aggregate numbers left the private directory (LLP 0018#processors).
- **Re-telling generalizes.** 36.5% of the lasting things people said after the cut they had already said before it
  (392 of 1,073). The share varies by person: 13% to 58%.
- **Asked on cue, the memories built before the cut knew:**

  | memory at the cut | pooled, 392 questions | mean over 18 accounts |
  |---|---|---|
  | what the product's prompt builder injects | 39.7% | 37.8% |
  | the product's stored memory, searched | 44.3% | 38.9% |
  | FluidDB facts + log | 58.4% | 59.2% |
  | FluidDB with routing | 56.1% | 58.7% |
  | the whole history, read at once | **70.0%** | **68.8%** |

- **FluidDB knows more than the product's memory,** by 16.5 points over what it injects (95% CI 12.2 to 20.7) and
  11.9 over everything it stores (7.9 to 16.1). It is ahead in 15 of the 18 accounts.
- **Reading the whole history knows 13.9 points more than FluidDB** (10.5 to 17.6). Round 10's order holds for other
  people: whole history > FluidDB > the product's memory.
- **The product's memory shows less than it keeps.** Searching its session notes (the 8 that match, plus the last
  5) instead of injecting the last ten adds 8.3 points on the six largest accounts and 4.6 on all 18.
- **Real conversation leaves few rows.** Across the accounts, the planner wrote 2-91 rows from 25-150 windows (one
  account 442), mostly into one table. Compiled code wrote none.
- **FluidDB's routing added nothing over facts + log here** (-2.3, CI -5.6 to 0.9). The router was the OpenAI model
  standing in for Jev (LLP 0018#processors).
- **The round cost about $7.66,** all OpenAI, estimated from logged tokens. No request went to any other provider.

## The extension: all 18 accounts

LLP 0018#amendment-all-qualifying-accounts. The other 12 accounts are smaller: 25-66 windows before the cut and 5-12
chats after.

| | six | twelve more | all 18 |
|---|---|---|---|
| re-told share of lasting statements | 40% | 33% | 36.5% |
| re-told questions | 206 | 186 | 392 |
| FluidDB routed minus what the product injects | +11.4 | +22.0 | **+16.5** (12.2 to 20.7) |
| FluidDB routed minus the stored memory, searched | +3.2 | +21.5 | **+11.9** (7.9 to 16.1) |
| the whole history minus FluidDB routed | +15.0 | +12.6 | **+13.9** (10.5 to 17.6) |
| the stored memory searched minus what is injected | +8.3 | +0.5 | +4.6 (1.7 to 7.5) |

- **Account by account:** FluidDB beats what the product injects in 15 of 18 (1 tie) and its stored memory in 15 of
  18 (2 ties). The whole history beats FluidDB in 12 of 18 (2 ties).
- **Where the product had no session notes at the cut** (u8, u22), it knew almost nothing: 0-17%. With notes, it
  knew 13-69%, with no clear relation to how many notes it had.
- **"Stored, searched" isn't a superset of "injected".** It shows the topics, the saved notes, and the 8 session
  notes that match the question plus the last 5. The injected memory shows the last 10 notes. With 13 notes or
  fewer, the injected memory can show more of them, which is why it sometimes scores higher.

## Hypotheses

LLP 0018#hypotheses, judged on the six; the 18 as the extension.
- **H1 (≥ 20% re-told): supported.** 40% on the six, 36.5% on the 18.
- **H2 (whole history > FluidDB > what the product injects; +10 and +5): supported.**
  - Six: 70.1 > 55.1 > 43.7, +11.4 and +15.0.
  - 18: +16.5 and +13.9.
- **H3 (the stored memory searched ≥ what is injected + 5): supported on the six** (+8.3), not on the 18 (+4.6).
- **H4 (the largest table ≥ 50% of rows, compiled < 10%): supported on the six** (6 of 6; compiled 0%). Half of
  the 12 others.
- **H5 (the gap ≥ 10 points larger on Czech): not testable on the six** (3 statements). Not supported on the 18:
  +6.4.

## What this changes (LLP 0015)

- **Round 10's findings hold beyond one person.** People re-tell about a third of what they say. A memory that
  searches the raw conversation knows 12-17 points more than what the product's memory shows, and reading the whole
  history knows 14 more still.
- **For a person's history up to ~85k tokens, reading it all recalls the most,** in 12 of 18 accounts.
  - It costs about $0.008 per read with Luna.
  - Within a session, the history is a stable prefix that providers cache, which lowers the cost further [inferred].
  - Beyond the context window, a memory is needed. FluidDB's log search is the best measured here.
- **Search the session notes instead of injecting the last ten.** For the product it is the cheapest gain: +8 points
  on the six largest accounts and +4.6 on all 18, with no new model.
- **The facts tables matter little for conversation.** They hold a few rows per account. The value sits in the log
  and the statements derived from it (LLP 0017.000#what-this-changes-in-the-design-llp-0015).

## Deviations

- **Before the guard existed,** a test command started the gold step for u8. It called only the OpenAI model, an
  allowed processor, and was stopped within two minutes. Its cached calls were reused.
- **The six accounts' cut was reduced before any export** (300 → 150 windows; 20 → 15 chats after), for the budget.
- **u5's gold hit its $0.30 guard** and was rerun with a higher one after the maintainer lifted the limits.
- **The extension to all 18 accounts was decided after five of the six were scored.** It is reported separately.
- **Two bugs were fixed during the round:**
  - The periods store assumed a `people` table, which one account lacked.
  - `real_return cue` still added control questions, which the RFC had dropped; they were removed before any memory
    run.
- **The Czech detector gained function words** after the six showed only 3 Czech statements under round 10's
  diacritics rule.
- **The spend was about $7.66** against the RFC's $4, after the maintainer lifted the limits.

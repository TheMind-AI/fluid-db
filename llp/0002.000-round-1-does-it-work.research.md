## Question

In 2023-24 FluidDB never worked reliably with GPT-4. The round asked five things:
- Does it work with 2026 models and Jev?
- Is the database it builds correct?
- How do you query it, and how fast is each way?
- Could it run in production?

[confirmed] (Adam Zvada, 2026-09-24) the user asked to "explore … and do experiments" with the new models and Jev.

## Setup

- **Data:**
  - the 2024 eval (`alex_rivera`, 90 questions)
  - a harder 7-week `life_stream` with corrections, deletions, receipts and emails (80 questions)
  - later, a Czech translation
- **Systems:**
  - the 2024 SQL pipeline ported as-is (`deprecated/python/lab/systems/legacy2024.py`)
  - FluidDB v2, where the model writes typed operations and an engine applies them; variants v2, v2jev, v21,
    v21jev
- **Models:** GPT-4-turbo (the 2024 baseline), GPT-6 Luna, Claude Sonnet 5, Claude Haiku 4.5, and Claude Opus 5.5 as
  the judge.

## What we learned

- **Don't let the model write SQL.** Typed operations plus a deterministic engine removed every write failure.
- **Show the planner the catalog and the related existing rows.** Most design mistakes (a second "David") come from
  not seeing what exists.
- **Keep the raw log.** Structure plus log beats either alone.
- **Third-party content must not change data:** documents may add, never overwrite or delete.
- **Jev fits closed, row-level decisions;** schema-level judgments need an LLM or a human.

## Decisions

- Typed operations and the engine (LLP 0004), the gate and source guard (LLP 0005), and GPT-6 Luna as the default
  writer.

## Caveats

- **Small datasets.** v2.1's rules were tuned on `life_stream` failures, so `alex_rivera` is the cleaner hold-out.
- **The structure audit used one Opus judge.**
